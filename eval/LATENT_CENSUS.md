Per (program, mode) the collector runs `sync_dominance.analyze` on every kernel dump exactly as
`run_cuvein._analyze_reports` does (same dot trial order, same `(pc pair, space)` dedup), in a child
process under a wall-clock and address-space cap, and keeps **every** verdict (not only RACE). For
each vector-clock dump it also runs the **same-trace** variant: the dump with `hb_races` and
`hb_races_sync_only` removed, which is exactly what a scalar-clock dump lacks, so `analyze` takes its
scalar-clock path (offline barrier pass, `CUVEIN_BARRIER_PASS_MAX_LANES` cutoff included) on the
same events. The script observes the detector without modifying it: it subclasses `HBGraph` to keep
the kernel's graph and shadows `sync_dominance`'s `json` name to keep (or, for the same-trace variant,
strip) the loaded dump.

For each pair without a static certificate it records:

- **R3 decline reason**: `HBGraph.ordered` / `chain` replayed step by step. The first gate the pair
  failed, in chain order:
  - `copy`
  - `cs-unfenced`: `_cs_fenced`, a plain store in a CAS section with no fence after the CAS.
  - `no-release-point`: no atomic follows the access in R3's dominance program order.
  - `no-release-fence`: no MEMBAR in postdom(access) ∩ dom(atomic).
  - `missing-hop`: no observed atomic–atomic hop within the release fence's scope.
  - `dominance`: hops land, but not on an atomic that dominates the other access.
  - `past-release`: `_past_release`.
- **Hand-offs**: observed atomic sync edges from one access's release side to an acquire before the
  other access. They are `certified` when they lie in R3's dominance program order. Otherwise the list
  falls back to hops that are merely CFG-reachable, marked `(reach)`.
- **Fence status per hand-off RMW**: Definition "Gate" (PTX gate) evaluated on the kernel CFG. `rel(r)`
  holds iff, with the MEMBARs of scope ≥ scope(r) deleted, no memory pc other than r reaches r; `acq(r)`
  holds iff none is reachable from r. This classification is not wired into any verdict.

Three groups. Each gets one sentence on why the race is hidden in the recorded schedule:

- **Eight fence and scope bugs** (all rows except the two rtraw ones). The hand-off completed in the
  run, and the engine joins every same-location RMW chain with the trusting gate (I4). A missing or
  under-scoped fence therefore does not break the vector-clock ordering. R3 checks fences, so it
  refuses to certify, and `latent` is what remains. In each of the eight, the ungated side found by
  the gate adjacency is exactly the side where the source omits or narrows the fence.
- **`race_interblock_fence_rtraw` is a race of the recorded run** that the engine missed through I1
  (tick before publish). In `evcand/.../vector-clock/kernel_0.json`, block 0 releases at seq 5
  (`atomicExch(&flag,1)`, pc 0x1e0), block 1's spin succeeds at seq 6 (pc 0x90), block 0 reads
  `data` at seq 7 (pc 0x1f0, after its own release) and block 1 writes `data` at seq 8 (pc 0x100).
  This is exactly `R_miss` of Proposition "Missed class of I1": a non-RMW record after the releaser's
  last tick, met before the acquirer's access. `hb_races` is empty. Only `latent` reports it.
  (Its hand-off is also acquire-ungated — no fence after the spin — so a PTX gate would leave the pair
  unordered as well.)
- **`race_interblock_none-lock_rtraw` is the only latent-only race whose recorded run genuinely orders
  it.** In the dump, block 1 acquires at seq 0, reads `data` at seq 3 (pc 0x140) and releases at seq 7
  (pc 0x1b0). Block 0's CAS then succeeds at seq 8 (pc 0x230), and it writes `data` after its own
  unlock at seq 13 (pc 0x340). Both RMWs are fence-adjacent. The race exists only in the schedule where
  block 0 locks first, which B1 item 1 works through. R3 declines through the past-release gate.

Source of the rtraw kernel (`ScoR/microbenchmarks/src/race_interblock_none-lock_rtraw.cu:19-41`;

__global__ void kmain(volatile unsigned int *data) {
    if(blockIdx.x == 0) {
        while(atomicCAS(&lock, 0, 1) != 0) {}
        __threadfence();
        dummy = data[0];
        __threadfence();
        atomicExch(&lock, 0);

## 6. Sanity: scalar-clock = vector-clock minus vetoes (§5 fact (i))

- **Same trace, 558 programs: 1 mismatch.** On 557 vector-clock dumps, the scalar-clock path (engine
  keys removed) reports exactly the vector-clock Race set minus the vetoes. A veto is a vector-clock
  RACE on a pair a static certificate orders: `model_bug`, or `structural` with an R3 chain; there
  are 151 vetoed report keys, 142 of them crs-cuda's `model_bug`. The mismatch is `P9-crs-cuda`, and its
  cause is the `CUVEIN_BARRIER_PASS_MAX_LANES` cutoff, one of the three failure modes the brief lists:
  - 26 of its kernels exceed 5 M lane-accesses, so the scalar-clock path skips the offline barrier
    pass. With no barrier-only set it also has no event-stream candidates.
  - Six vector-clock `structural` WAR pairs in `kernel_10.json` (5,636,268 lane-accesses; shared memory,
    `kernels.cu:51` vs `:34/36/43`) have no dependency edge, so they exist only as event candidates.
    Vector-clock mode reports them; scalar-clock mode never judges them.
  - Scalar-clock mode misses what vector-clock mode reports there. Here that costs nothing, because
    these are crs-cuda's exit-unaware-barrier false positives (T3). On a racy kernel of that size it
    would be a scalar-clock false negative.

  The other two failure modes did not occur: an absent `hb_races_sync_only` key, and a
  candidate-generation difference (engine `hb_races` pairs outside the offline barrier set). Wherever
  both ran, the offline barrier pass and the engine's second clock agree pair-for-pair.
- **Own run, 558 programs: 12 differ**: crs-cuda (the same cutoff gap) and 11 others (9 P1 `…Push…ReadWrite…RaceBug_Thread` programs,
  `P4-graph-coloring-racy-small`, `P6-memcpy-global_readwrite_race-racy`; list in census.md). The scalar-clock mode is a separate
  GPU recording, so its schedule and events differ, and 1–2 report keys move between the two sides. No
  program's verdict flips between the modes because of it: all 12 are RACE in both. Over the 558
  programs with both dumps, the only program whose verdict differs between the modes is `P5-canary`
  (vector-clock RACE, scalar-clock CLEAN). That is a veto, the case fact (i) describes.

## 7. Findings for T6 / T9 (facts, no detector change)

1. **Definition "Gate" and R3's `_cs_fenced` both reject the "fence on the success branch" acquire
   idiom.** In `matrix-multiplication-norace` SASS (`mm_kernel.sm_89.dot`), `ATOMG.E.CAS.STRONG.SM` at 0x540
   is followed by `ISETP; BRA`. The success block has `MEMBAR.SC.CTA` at 0x580 before the protected
   `LDG/STG`. The failure edge leaves through `.L_x_4 → .L_x_3 → BB4 → .L_x_7`, out of the do-while and
   on to later memory pcs with no fence. That path is infeasible at runtime (a failed CAS never
   proceeds to them without a later successful CAS) but feasible in the CFG. So `acq(0x540) = 0` under
   the PTX gate as written, and `release_scope(CAS, x)` finds no fence in postdom(CAS). The same holds for
   reduction's ticket `atomicInc` (acquire side ungated; ordering completed by `__syncthreads()`). A
   PTX-gated engine (I4 fixed) would therefore turn the hand-offs of these race-free programs into
   unordered pairs: structural FPs in place of today's latent ones. *Tested on the SASS of one
   kernel; the PTX gate itself was not implemented or run.*
2. **`race_interblock_fence_rtraw` is a corpus instance of I1's `R_miss` in a recorded trace.**
   CLAUDE.md B1 and §7 of `hb_proof.tex` say "the miss has not shown in the corpus yet". The kept evcand
   vector-clock dump shows it: the engine misses the race, and the verdict survives only through
   `latent`. *Proved-in-effect on this one trace (event order read from the dump), not re-run with a
   publish-then-tick oracle.*

## 8. Reading for D8 (no recommendation)

On the recorded runs, the `latent` tier is responsible for:

- 10 of 236 vector-clock true positives, all ScoR litmus. Three of them no baseline catches.
- 3 of 20 false-positive programs (25 of 50 latent reports), all ScoR apps.

Under "ordered in this execution" with today's engine, all 10 become false negatives and the 3
programs become clean. The census also says why the 10 were hidden:

- eight because the engine's gate trusts fences it does not check (I4);
- one because of I1 (`R_miss`);
- one, the rtraw lock, because the schedule genuinely ordered it.

So what D8 costs depends on D1 and on the gate:

- With D1 applied, `fence_rtraw` would be `structural` in this trace.
- With a PTX gate, the eight fence and scope races would be unordered in the run, but so would the
  success-branch-fenced hand-offs of Finding 1, on race-free programs.
- `none-lock_rtraw` stays reportable only under "ordered in every schedule", or through a re-recording
  in the other schedule.

These projections are hand analysis of the census fields, not measured with a modified detector.

## 9. What remains unverified

- The fence status is the adjacency condition of Definition "Gate" evaluated on nvdisasm CFGs from the
  kept `dots/`. It was checked by hand against the source for the ten latent-only kernels and one SASS
  listing (matrix-multiplication), not against a PTX-gated engine. `(reach)` hand-offs are CFG-reachable
  observed hops, not proven to be the hand-off the engine used.
- The R3 decline reason is a replay of `HBGraph.chain`. It was cross-checked against the verdicts only
  in the sense that no replay returned `chain` for a latent pair (a pair R3 certifies is never latent),
  not by an independent implementation.
- The projections in §8 (D1 applied, PTX gate) are hand analysis, not re-scores.
- Coverage: no vector-clock dump exists for P7, 8 of 9 P9 programs, and the vector-clock timeouts in
  P1/P3/P4. Latent pairs there are unknown. PI has no kept traces at all.
- Labels are suite labels (P5 litmus names, ScoR `RACEY` builds). A P4 `norace` build is taken as
  race-free by its label, not by an oracle.
